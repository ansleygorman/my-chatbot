## What distracted driving means
Distracted driving is any activity that takes a driver's attention away from operating a vehicle safely. It includes using a phone, eating, drinking, talking to passengers, or adjusting the radio or GPS. Even activities that seem harmless can increase the risk of a crash when they take attention away from the road.
##Why texting while driving is especially dangerous
Texting combines three types of distraction: visual, manual, and cognitive. Drivers take their eyes off the road, their hands off the steering wheel, and their minds off driving. Reading or sending a text takes about five seconds, which at 55 mph is equivalent to driving the length of a football field without looking at the road.
##Distracted driving deaths and injuries
In 2024, 3,208 people were killed in crashes involving distracted drivers, and more than 315,000 people were injured. These numbers show how distracted driving can affect not only the driver but also passengers, pedestrians, cyclists, and other people on the road.
##Phone use behind the wheel
Using a phone while driving includes texting, making calls, scrolling through social media, checking notifications, and using apps. Drivers should not assume that hands-free phone use is completely safe because conversations can still take their mental attention away from driving.
##Distracted driving laws and consequences
Texting while driving is illegal in 49 states, as well as Washington, DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and the Virgin Islands. Drivers who violate distracted-driving laws may face fines or other penalties. NHTSA's campaign emphasizes that drivers can face legal consequences as well as the possibility of causing a serious crash.
##How to safely respond to messages
If a driver needs to read or send a text, they should first pull over to a safe location and park. A passenger can also act as the designated texter by responding to messages or helping with navigation. Drivers should avoid trying to respond while stopped temporarily at a traffic light.
##Ways to prevent phone distractions
Drivers can activate their phone's Do Not Disturb feature, silence notifications, or put their phone somewhere they cannot easily reach, such as the trunk. These actions help prevent the temptation to check messages while driving and allow the driver to focus on the road.
##How passengers and friends can help
Passengers should avoid distracting the driver and can help by handling phone calls, messages, or navigation. If someone notices a friend texting while driving, they should speak up and encourage them to put their phone away. NHTSA encourages everyone to commit to driving without distractions and to help others make safer choices.
